How to Measure Your HVAC Filter — Step by Step

Measuring your HVAC filter correctly takes 60 seconds and prevents the frustrating experience of ordering the wrong size. Here is the exact process used by HVAC professionals:

  • Step 1: Locate your filter. It is usually in the return air vent (a large grate on a wall or ceiling) or in a slot on the air handler itself.
  • Step 2: Remove the existing filter. If it has a label, the nominal size is printed there — you are done. Common format: '20x25x1' or '16x25x1'.
  • Step 3: If there is no label, measure the filter's length, width, and depth with a tape measure. Round each dimension UP to the nearest inch — this is your nominal size.
  • Step 4: Double-check by measuring the slot opening itself. Your nominal filter size should be approximately 0.25–0.5 inches larger than the actual measured dimensions.
  • Step 5: Record the size. Write it on the inside of your furnace door, or photograph the old filter before discarding it.

Nominal vs Actual Size — The Confusing Industry Standard

Every HVAC filter is labeled in nominal dimensions — the rounded-up marketing size. The actual filter you receive is approximately 0.25–0.5 inches smaller in each dimension. A 20x25x1 nominal filter typically measures 19.75x24.75x0.75 inches. This industry convention exists to ensure filters fit snugly in their labeled slot sizes.

Finding Non-Standard Sizes — What to Do When Your Size Is Unusual

If your HVAC uses a non-standard filter size like 17x22x1 or 19x21x1, you have a few options. Several online retailers specialize in custom filter cutting: Nordic Pure, FilterBuy, and Second Nature all offer made-to-order filters at minor premiums over standard sizes.

Before ordering custom, double-check your measurement. Non-standard sizes are often due to measurement error — the actual filter slot is a standard size but the old filter was slightly undersized. Try ordering one of the five most common sizes closest to your measurement and see if it fits.

How Sizing Affects Filtration Performance

A correctly-sized filter seals the entire air handler inlet, forcing all airflow through the filter media. A filter that is even half an inch too small allows a significant gap through which unfiltered air can bypass entirely. Studies have shown that a 1/4-inch gap around a MERV 13 filter reduces effective filtration to roughly MERV 4 performance because most airflow takes the path of least resistance through the gap.

This is why filter fit matters at least as much as MERV rating. A properly-fitted MERV 8 filter will outperform a MERV 13 filter with even a small gap every time. When in doubt, size up rather than down — a very slightly oversized filter can be trimmed with scissors; an undersized filter cannot be made to seal.

How to Read the Arrow on Your HVAC Filter

Every quality HVAC filter has an airflow direction arrow printed on the cardboard frame. This arrow must point toward the air handler (in the direction of airflow). Installing a filter backwards reduces its efficiency because the media is engineered with finer filtration layers on the downstream side — installing backwards puts the fine layer first, which clogs faster and increases static pressure more rapidly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is nominal size vs actual size for HVAC filters?

Nominal size (e.g., 20x25x1) is the labeled size you order. Actual size is slightly smaller (~0.25–0.5 inches per dimension, e.g., 19.75x24.75x0.75). Always order by nominal size.

How do I measure my HVAC filter size?

Remove the existing filter and read the size printed on its cardboard frame. If there is no label, measure length x width x depth of the filter slot and round up to the nearest inch.

What are the most common HVAC filter sizes?

20x25x1, 16x25x1, 20x20x1, 16x20x1, and 14x25x1 are the five most common residential sizes. 20x25x4 is the most common 4-inch media filter size.

What if my filter size is not standard?

Custom filter sizes are available from FilterBuy, Nordic Pure, and several other online vendors who cut filters to any size. Expect 3–7 day lead time and 30–50% premium over standard sizes.

Can I use a slightly wrong-size filter temporarily?

A filter that is too small leaves gaps that allow unfiltered air to bypass entirely. A filter that is too large will not fit. Never use the wrong size — gaps completely defeat filtration.

What does the third number in filter size mean?

The third number is the filter thickness: 20x25x1 means 20 inches wide, 25 inches tall, 1 inch thick. Common thicknesses: 1, 2, 4, and 5 inches.

Do all filter brands sell the same sizes?

Major brands (Filtrete, Honeywell, Nordic Pure) cover the 20 most common sizes. For unusual sizes, Nordic Pure and FilterBuy offer the widest selection and custom cutting.

How do I find my filter size if I cannot remove the filter?

Check your HVAC system's manual or look up your model number online. The filter size is usually listed in the installation specs. Alternatively, look for a label on the air handler near the filter slot.

What happens if I put the filter in backwards?

Most filters have an airflow arrow printed on the frame. Installing backwards reduces efficiency — the media is engineered to work in one direction. Always match the arrow to your duct's airflow direction (toward the air handler).

Is a 16x20x1 and a 20x16x1 the same filter?

By convention, filter sizes are listed width x height x depth, but retailers often list the longer dimension first. A 16x20x1 and 20x16x1 are the same filter. If in doubt, measure your slot.
